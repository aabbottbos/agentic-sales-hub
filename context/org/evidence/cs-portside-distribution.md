# Portside Distribution

## The pain

Portside had **already failed once**. A software-only visibility vendor spent
nine months trying to integrate Portside's customized NetSuite instance and two
WMS systems, never got past integration, and the project was written off. The
distributor still had the underlying problem: no shared view of order status
across six DCs, and a legacy routing tool nobody could get data out of.

## What we did

We scoped the SOW **around the integration risk**, not around a standard
template:

- A 10-week integration phase (vs. the typical 4–8), sized for the customized
  NetSuite instance and both WMS systems.
- The legacy routing-tool connector carved out as an **explicit change-order
  path** — priced separately, so the base SOW wasn't hostage to the riskiest
  component.
- Discovery produced a written integration design that Portside's IT team signed
  off on before any build started.

## Results

- **Live in 16 weeks** after nine months of zero progress with the prior vendor.
- **Same-day order-exception resolution rose from 51% to 79%.**
- **$1.2M ARR expansion** within 12 months: three more DCs from an acquisition,
  plus analytics-API seats for the merchandising team.

## Why this deal is a good proof point

It is the "we tried visibility software and it didn't work" objection, answered.
The difference was a services engagement scoped to the specific integration
risk, with the scariest connector on its own change order.
